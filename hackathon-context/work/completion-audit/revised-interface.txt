THREE REMAINING MATERIAL RISKS

1. HIGH — Source authority and accepted measurement remain unverified dependencies.
The three-control path requires an independent expected-work source across all zones, authoritative actual model usage including retries, and accepted diagnostic labels/output mapping. A complete architecture cannot supply these facts by itself. The revised gate branches are credible, but one accepted demonstration scenario is not proof of whole-scope population coverage. Require each source probe to produce a scope/coverage argument, real payload and negative/missingness case; obtain organizer acceptance for any reference corpus or recorded breach. If those fail, keep the declared2-control or detection-only ceiling. D2/D6 and W1/W5 remain3 for this reason, not because production evidence was expected from a design-only task.

2. HIGH — Safe prevention depends on faithfully implementing the stated transaction boundary.
The protocol is now coherent, but a developer can easily split the run-state check, dispatch ownership or settlement updates into separate operations, defeating it. Add a named dispatch-versus-close race to the existing acceptance campaign and inject crashes after every transition commit but before the next external effect. Verify hidden SDK/provider retries: if an admitted attempt can trigger multiple billable provider executions, source reconciliation must expose all of them and the strict bound may be invalid. Verify the outbox and ingestion acknowledgment survive restart. These are implementation/integration acceptance risks, not remaining contradictory requirements; they do not justify calling the current design deployed or tested.

3. MEDIUM — Two-person delivery remains sensitive to integration work.
RoleB owns the riskiest data experiment, ServiceNow, export/query and reference checker. RoleA owns all source normalization, event obligations and runtime state. This is plausible only if the initial gates expose usable sources quickly. Preserve the90-minute discovery timebox as a decision point, not a promise that provisioning finishes. After discovery, move specific finished work to the less-blocked person and stop optional state-machine/second-adapter work until required artifacts are complete. No additional agent service should compete with accepted baseline construction or actual AICT evidence. The cut lines are now explicit, but throughput and permissions remain untested.

V2 closes the two must-fix contradictions identified in v1: dispatch now checks OPEN atomically against closure, and Control7 includes admission.error without inventing denial evidence. Already-dispatching calls remain liabilities; evidence finality explicitly means an as-of cutoff, not proof every remote request finished. No remaining must-fix source contradiction found in v2. Four transports, off-path register/ledger, pure evaluator plus separate coordinator, honest breach evidence and out-of-scope cryptographic sealing align with the slides.
